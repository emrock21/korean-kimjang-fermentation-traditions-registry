# Korean Kimjang Fermentation Traditions Registry

This repository preserves, in an immutable and decentralized format, the fermentation traditions of **Kimjang** — the communal preparation of kimchi, recognized by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Kimjang is not merely food preparation: it is a **seasonal ritual**, a **social institution**, and a **microbial technology**. Families and neighbors gather in late autumn to prepare large quantities of kimchi for winter, using regional ingredients and fermentation techniques passed down through generations.

## Why Kimjang Matters

Kimjang represents:

- a **communal cultural practice**, strengthening social bonds  
- a **regional fermentation science**, with distinct microbial profiles  
- a **culinary heritage**, foundational to Korean identity  
- a **seasonal ritual**, marking the transition into winter  
- a **UNESCO-recognized tradition**, inscribed in 2013  

Each region of Korea has its own kimchi types, ingredients, and fermentation methods.

## Purpose of This Contract

The goal of this registry is to:

1. **Create a permanent, censorship-resistant record** of Kimjang traditions.  
2. **Preserve endangered fermentation knowledge** with technical accuracy.  
3. **Document ingredients, techniques, seasonal context, and cultural meaning**.  
4. **Provide an immutable reference** for researchers, chefs, and future generations.  
5. **Honor the regional diversity of Korean kimchi-making.**

The contract is intentionally:

- **ownerless** — no authority can alter or censor the registry  
- **payment-free** — no monetization, no barriers  
- **minimalistic and secure** — no external calls  
- **permanent** — entries become part of the cultural ledger  

## What Each Entry Records

- **kimchiType** — baechu, dongchimi, bossam, gat, yeolmu  
- **region** — Jeolla, Gyeongsang, Hwanghae, Pyongan  
- **ingredients** — cabbage, radish, gochugaru, jeotgal, pear, garlic  
- **techniques** — salting, brining, seasoning, fermentation  
- **seasonalContext** — winter kimjang, summer varieties  
- **culturalContext** — communal preparation, family roles  
- **uniqueness** — regional identity, fermentation profile
